Moonshot AI publishes Kimi K3's full open weights, the largest open-weight release ever

Moonshot AI released the complete 2.8-trillion-parameter weights for Kimi K3 - a mixture-of-experts model that activates roughly 50B parameters per token and topped Arena.ai's Frontend Code Arena leaderboard. The download is about 1.4TB, so most teams will run it via inference providers rather than self-hosting until further-quantized versions appear.

SK Group and Nvidia sign $500B+ partnership spanning AI factories and next-gen memory

The companies signed letters of intent covering a 2-gigawatt AI Cloud in Korea built on Nvidia's DSX platform and Vera Rubin accelerators powered by SK hynix HBM4, targeted to come online in 2027. Nvidia and SK hynix also agreed to a long-term partnership to secure and codevelop next-generation AI memory, including future HBM generations.

Read more at Tom's Hardware

A single downed power line briefly destabilized the US East Coast grid via AI data centers

When a power line failed outside Washington, DC, more than 3 gigawatts of Northern Virginia data centers dropped off the grid nearly simultaneously as their backup systems reacted to the voltage dip, sending a voltage disturbance across PJM's territory from Virginia to Chicago. No blackout resulted, but the incident underscores how concentrated AI data center demand (already 6% of PJM's load, projected to hit 24% by 2040) has become a systemic grid risk.

QuantiBias: Benchmarking Quantization-Induced Bias in LLMs

Quantized models still pass standard safety checks - refusing harmful prompts and picking unbiased multiple-choice answers - yet on open-ended questions the same models volunteer stereotypes in roughly one in four answers across eight languages, a side effect standard evaluation misses entirely.

Representation Robustness Under Executable Reasoning Constraints in LLMs for Mathematical Problem Solving

Testing models across equivalent formulations of the same math problems - story form, word-equations, symbolic equations, and paraphrases - the authors find substantial "flip rates," where a model gets a problem right in one representation and wrong in another, suggesting some reported reasoning failures are really interface failures.
