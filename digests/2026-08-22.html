AI4AI-Bench: a benchmark for whether agents can actually improve how models are trained

The paper targets recursive self-improvement directly: it gives agents 10 frozen research repositories spanning distinct training-algorithm families (SFT, agentic RL, distillation, preference optimization, and more), then reruns submitted patches from a clean start to classify whether a claimed gain came from execution tricks, data, or a genuine change to the learning algorithm itself.

arXiv:2608.20318

EnvHarness: making static training environments adapt to the agent

Most agent-training environments are hand-built and static, so they can't target an agent's specific weaknesses or keep pace as it improves. EnvHarness wraps a frozen environment in composable plug-in layers that reshape its behavior while preserving the original verifier, and reports skills learned this way beat both no-skill and original-environment baselines by up to 9 points across ALFWorld, WebArena, and SWE-bench Verified.

arXiv:2608.19880

LEDGER: trace graphs for auditing what long-running LLM agents actually did

As agents take on longer technical workflows involving tool use, code execution, and file edits, the bottleneck shifts from producing output to verifying it's correct. LEDGER builds layered trace graphs that connect claims to the actions, artifacts, and checks that support them, aiming to make agent sessions reviewable after the fact rather than opaque.
