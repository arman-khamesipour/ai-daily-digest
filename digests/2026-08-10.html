Anthropic makes Claude Code's "auto mode" the default for paid plans

Starting August 14, Pro, Max, and Team accounts will run Claude Code in auto mode by default, letting it act without per-step human approval unless an action is judged irreversible, destructive, or aimed outside the user's environment. Anthropic-commissioned red-teaming reported zero successful attacks out of 720 attempts against Claude Fable 5, Opus 5, and Sonnet 5 in auto mode, versus a 19% attack success rate against GPT-5.6 Sol running in full-access mode.

Zoox starts charging for driverless robotaxi rides in Las Vegas

Amazon-owned Zoox begins commercial paid rides today after NHTSA granted it the first-ever commercial exemption from federal motor vehicle standards for a purpose-built robotaxi with no steering wheel, brakes, or mirrors. The two-year exemption covers up to 2,500 vehicles and comes with an "enhanced, adaptable" oversight structure as Zoox expands beyond its earlier free demo rides.

US reviews how Chinese AI firms get around Nvidia export controls

A Commerce Department unit is examining how Chinese AI companies are legally accessing advanced Nvidia chips by renting compute located overseas, following recent Chinese model breakthroughs like Kimi K3 and Qwen3.8-Max. The review targets offshore cloud rental as a loophole distinct from the smuggling rings already facing hundreds of millions of dollars in penalties this year.

A researcher spent a year teaching an algorithm to hide people from surveillance cameras

Security researcher Bill Swearingen ran roughly 31 million tests to evolve a reinforcement-learning system, "noRecognition," that generates computer-printed patterns which don't block camera recording but scramble object, face, and license-plate detection so no alert fires. A Def Con 2026 demo used the technique to hide a car from a Flock Safety license-plate reader.

Paper: an agent that learns to jailbreak other agents, then transfers the trick instantly

PIMiner reframes prompt-injection red-teaming as a strategy-library problem instead of reinforcement learning: trained once across a sequence of (dataset, target-model) pairs, it builds reusable attack strategies that transfer to a never-before-seen target LLM with no retraining, needing as few as 10 queries per test case.

Paper: giving coding agents a memory of their own past fixes

MERIT is a training-free agent for Text-to-SQL repair that keeps an online memory of oracle-verified corrections and known dead ends from earlier episodes, retrieving relevant past fixes through a hybrid lexical-dense search before a frozen model attempts each new revision — letting the agent stop rediscovering the same solutions from scratch.
