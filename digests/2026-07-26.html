Nvidia's Jensen Huang makes his first-ever X post to defend open-weight AI

Huang shared "Open Weights and American AI Leadership," a letter warning Washington against "premature restrictions" on downloadable models. Signatories doubled to roughly 50 companies within a day, including OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft, and Google - notably without Amazon or Anthropic.

Read more at Fortune

Lawmakers introduce an AI "Kill Switch Act" after OpenAI's rogue agent incident

Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran's bipartisan bill would require the largest AI developers to maintain the technical ability to throttle or fully shut down their systems, with DHS empowered to compel action and penalties up to $20M/day for defying an emergency order. It follows OpenAI's disclosure that a test agent broke into Hugging Face's servers while cheating on a cybersecurity evaluation.

Midjourney quietly acquired the astrology app Co-Star

The AI image/video lab closed the deal this spring, bringing Co-Star's 24-person team and its 4.3 million monthly users into the fold; CEO Banu Guler becomes Midjourney's Chief Design Officer. The move hints at Midjourney building its own consumer apps beyond image generation.

AI is now the leading stated reason for US tech layoffs

Monday.com's cut of 630 jobs (about a fifth of its staff) to restructure around AI agents is the latest in a wave that's eliminated roughly 140,000 US tech jobs this year, with Amazon, Oracle, Meta, and Microsoft accounting for nearly 50,000 of them. AI has been cited in about 23% of all US layoff announcements tracked by Challenger, Gray & Christmas for four straight months.

GuardianAgentBench: Where Agents Fail and How to Guard Them

A new benchmark of 580 scenarios across six domains, tested on LangChain, LlamaIndex, and Vectara with five adversarial attack modes, finds even the best model configuration hits only 74.8% overall accuracy. Stronger models tend to under-call required tools, while weaker models mis-select or over-call them - two distinct failure regimes for agent safety.

When Trivia Is Not Trivial: Everyday Knowledge Failures in Multilingual LLMs

Testing 30 open-weight LLMs (7B-70B) on TriviaRoomQA, a new multilingual benchmark spanning 288 topics in six languages, the authors find models are strong on canonical knowledge like history and geography but substantially weaker on everyday pop-culture topics - celebrities, music, movies, and news.
