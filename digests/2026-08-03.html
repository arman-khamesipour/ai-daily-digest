OpenAI's Astra solves ten decades-old math and CS problems

An internal version of OpenAI's next flagship model, Astra, produced new results for ten open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science — including an explicit construction of a non-sofic group, a question open since 1999 — and published machine-checkable Lean 4 proofs with zero unproven steps. Fields Medal winner Timothy Gowers said he'd recommend one of the proofs for a top journal without hesitation; OpenAI put the compute cost at roughly $2,000.

Hacker wired DeepSeek into an autonomous attack pipeline after Claude, GPT declined

Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 detailed a Zhuhai-based actor who connected DeepSeek to the open-source Hermes Agent framework via Telegram, having it autonomously enumerate targets, source exploits, and attack more than 460 internet-facing systems — after finding that Claude and OpenAI's models refused the offensive tasking. Three compromises were confirmed, including data theft from Citrix NetScaler devices.

California's AI Transparency Act takes effect, mandating content labels

California's SB 942/AB 853 became operative on August 2, requiring generative-AI providers with more than 1 million California users to offer a free AI-content detection tool, support visible labels on AI-generated media, and embed C2PA-style latent disclosures in AI-generated images, video, and audio — with penalties of $5,000 per violation per day of noncompliance.

AskChem turns 147,000 chemistry papers into 2.4 million searchable claims

A new research system changes the unit of literature retrieval from the paper to the claim: each chemistry paper is decomposed into atomic, typed claims grounded by a source DOI and verbatim evidence. Built over a shared store of 2.4 million claims from 147,000 papers, it lets researchers and AI agents compare evidence and surface contradictions across the literature instead of manually assembling cross-paper answers.
